## What are Ham radio callsigns?

Simply put, your Ham Radio callsign is your name on the air. It's your ID, almost like a phone number.<sup>*</sup>

In the United States, Ham radio callsigns start with letters K, N, or W, followed by a number 0-9.<sup><small>[2]</small></sup> They can have 1-2 prefix letters (before the number) and 1-3 letters after the number. Two-letter prefixes of AA-AL, KA-KZ, NA-NZ, WA-WZ can also be used.<sup><small>[2]</small></sup> List of the usable formats:
- 1x1: K9A <sup>**</sup>
- 1x2: K9AB
- 1x3: K9ABC
- 2x1: KA9A
- 2x2: KA9AB
- 2x3: KA9ABC

The number (0-9) comes from the district the callsign is in.<sup><small>[1]</small></sup> (Nowadays it is used loosely.) Callsigns are issued by the Federal Communications Commission, or FCC. 

### Vanity Callsigns
A vanity callsign is like a vanity plate for license plates. You can apply for any callsign as long as the callsign is available and you **have the required class.**

<sup>*</sup><small>(Digital Mobile Radios (DMRs) can have something like phone numbers but that's for a later article.)</small>

<sup>**</sup><small>(The 1x1 format is special, as it is issued by the ARRL, and not the FCC. It is a non-permanent assignment that usually lasts about 15 days upon request. More often than not, 1x1 callsigns are used for special events such as historical anniversaries.)</small>
